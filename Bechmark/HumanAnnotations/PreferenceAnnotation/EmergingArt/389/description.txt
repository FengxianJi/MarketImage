(*) Max Bill was a Swiss architect, painter, sculptor, graphic designer, typographer and industrialist, publicist and educator. 
He was born in Winterhur, Switzerland. From 1924 to 1927, he studied at the Kunstgewerbeschule in Zurich, where he was influenced by Dadaism and Cubism. From 1927 to 1929, he studies arts at the Bauhaus in Dessau, where he became interested in functionalism in design.  When he finished his studies, he returned to Zurich and dedicated himself to painting, architecture and graphic design. 
In 1930, he created his own architecture studio and, as a member of the Deutscher Werkbund, he designed the Nuebühl estate, near Zurich in the modern style. 
In 1931, he adopted the theory of ‘concrete art’ by Theo van Doesburg, according to which it was possible to reach universality with clarity. 
In 1932, he worked as a sculptor and joined some artistic organisations such as the Abstraction-Creation and the group of Swiss artists Allianz. 
In 1944, Bill moved into industrial design with the design of an aluminium clock on behalf of Junghans, a company with which he collaborated for years designed wall and kitchen clocks and wristwatches with a rationalist style and industrial aesthetics.  He organised the exhibition ‘Die gute Industrieform’ which aimed to encourage the creation of higher quality objects of industrial design. He also designed a minimalist stool, the Ulmer Hocker (1954), one of his best-known designs. 
In 1951, he founded the Hochschule für Gestaltung in Ulm, Germany, a school that continued the tradition of Bauhaus. In that institution, he was the vice-chancellor and director of the departments of architecture and production design between 1951 and 1956. In this school, Bill defended the functionalism of Bauhaus, because he believed that forms based in laws of mathematics possessed aesthetic purity, and therefore, universality. 
In 1957, after abandoning the HfG in Ulm, he founded his own studio in Zurich and dedicated himself to sculptor, painting and architecture. 
From 1967 to 1971, he was a member of the Swiss National Council, and then became professor at the Staatliche Hochschule für Bildende Künste in Hamburg. 
In 1976, he became a member of the Berlin Academy of Arts. 
Max Bill passed away in 1994, in Berlin.
