Title: Mercury and Aeneas in front of Carthage

Artist: Master of Grüninger Workshop (Fl. 1502)
Year: 1502
Technique: Woodcut print
Dimensions: 140×165×1 mm
Condition: Good condition

Description:
Woodcut from the "Strasbourg Vergilius", edited by Sebastian Brant: 
Publii Virgilii Maronis Opera cum quinque vulgatis commentariis expolitissimisque figureis atque imaginibus nuper per Sebastianum Brant superadditis (Strasbourg: Johannis Grieninger, 1502) 
One of the great German woodcut books of the Renaissance, and the masterpiece of the artist known as the Late Master of the Grüninger Workshop. 
Mercury, compelled by Jupiter, flies into Carthage wearing his winged shoes, carrying his caduceus and a traveler's pouch. He approaches Aeneas, who stands in the center, directing the construction of at least one building. A laborer chops a plank with an ax, there appears to be a brick mold next to the laborer. Aeneas has a sword and wears a cloak that looks quite opulent, both gifts from Dido. Mercury scolds Aeneas for being content to settle in Carthage with Dido instead of following his fate to found his own kingdom in Italy. 
Woodcut illustration from the “Strasbourg Vergil,” edited by Sebastian Brant: Publii Virgilii Maronis Opera cum quinque vulgatis commentariis expolitissimisque figuris atque imaginibus nuper per Sebastianum Brant superadditis (Strasbourg: Johannis Grieninger, 1502), executed by an anonymous engraver under the direction of Brant.
