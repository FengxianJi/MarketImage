Title: The fall of Phaeton

Artist: Antonio Tempesta (c.1655-1737)
Year: 1606
Technique: Etching
Dimensions: 1.75x2.25x0.01 cm
Condition: Good condition

Description:
Original etching by the hand Antonio Tempesta. 
One of the most important scenes of his masterpiece  : Phaetontis casus 
The fall of Phaeton, with the horses tumbling towards earth, Phaeton falling out of the chariot to left, and Jupiter on clouds above at centre, brandishing a thunderbolt. 
A personal note: The theme seems very topical today, with a humanity that misuses his power over the planet and its environment and thus causes fire and mischief wherever it touches the earth. 
From: 
Metamorphoseon sive transformationum 
A series of etchings done in 1606. 
Reference: 
Bartsch / Le Peintre graveur (XVII.151.649) 
106 x 120 mm on plate border. 
Condition: 
Good early impressions with little scratches in the plate still visible. Good wide margins In a full little sheet of laid paper from the period. Excellent condition with no folds tears or stains. 
Fine etching in the sensitive style of the Northern Italian Schools 
Engraved after his own designs by Antonio Tempesta (1555-1630) 
Painter-engraver from Florence. Important link between Flemish and Italian art. Pupil of Stradanus .His fresco of hunting and battle scenes are highly estimated. Antonio, perhaps even more then Rembrandt saw the etching technique as the medium to express his art. 
Tempesta is best known for his etchings for Ovid's Metamorphoses and his innovative prints of battles and hunts. (Source: MET museum)
