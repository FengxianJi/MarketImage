Born May 2, 1904 and died June 29, 2001 in Culan (Cher). He is one of the major painters of the new School of Paris. Excellent colorist, his style is characterized by an intertwining of forms whose natural, even organic qualities testify to a great poetry. Maurice Estève spent his childhood in Culan with his grandparents. He joined them in 1913 in Paris, where he discovered the Louvre Museum, particularly impressed by the Battle of San Roman by Paol Ucello, but also Courbet, Delacroix and Chardin. Returning to Culan for the summer he stayed there during the war years, began to paint in 1915. He lived again in Paris from 1918. Apprenticed to a typographer and then in a modern furniture drawing workshop, he attended evening drawing classes, discovered in 1919 the painting of Cézanne then, meeting the worried opposition of his father to his vocation, directed in 1923 in Barcelona, for a year, a workshop of drawing shawls and fabrics. Back in Paris Estève attends in Montparnasse the free workshop of the Colarossi Academy.
