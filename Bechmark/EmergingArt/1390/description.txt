Jacques de Gheyn II (1565-1629)
Draughtsman, engraver and publisher; born in Antwerp, his father being a glass-painter and print dealer. Trained with Goltzius in Haarlem 1585-90; then settled in Amsterdam; in 1592 de Gheyn began to publish prints himself, some after his own designs but still many after van Mander; like Goltzius, he gave up printmaking to paint in 1600.

De Gheyn promoted Holland's emancipation from Spain with the first systematic manual of how to use a pike, musket and other weapons. Count Jan van Nassau Siegen commissioned Jacques de Gheyn II in 1597 to design drawings for an engraved handbook on the exercise of arms for the infantry and cavalry. It was published in 1607 in They Hague, containing 117 superb engravings by De Gheyn himself and his coöperators, with a dedication to the States General.
